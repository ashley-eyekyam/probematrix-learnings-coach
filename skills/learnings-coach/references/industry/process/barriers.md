# Process industries: barriers and controls

Method reference, not evidence. These barriers are common in refining, chemicals, gas and storage sites. Use the words of `../../barriers-and-controls.md` (barrier, control, state, type, hierarchy of controls), and name a barrier in a pack only when the cited findings or evidence show it. What a barrier did on the day comes only from the export; this list never says it was present, degraded or absent.

Barrier states are those of the pack `barrier` kind: `absent`, `degraded`, `defeated`, `worked partly`, `worked`. When the export is silent, write "[GAP]".

## 1. Prevention barriers

| Barrier | Type | What it stops | Controls that keep it healthy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Design of containment and operating limits | passive | Material leaving primary containment in normal and upset conditions | Process safety information kept current; design basis recorded; material and corrosion allowances |
| Mechanical integrity programme | procedural | Thinning, cracking or seal failure reaching a leak | Inspection plan by risk; defect tracking to closure; fitness-for-service decisions; spares for critical items |
| Process control and alarm system | active | A process deviation growing into a hazardous state | Rationalised alarms; alarm response procedures; operator checks of alarm health; managed alarm changes |
| Safety instrumented functions and interlocks | active | A deviation passing its safe limit | Proof tests on schedule; bypass control with an expiry; independence from the control system |
| Overpressure protection (relief devices) | passive | Vessel or line overpressure | Set-point records; inspection and test of devices; no isolation valves left closed under a device |
| Permit-to-work and isolation | procedural | Work releasing material or energy into people | Hazard check at the site; proved isolation; lock and tag control; handback tests; permit audit |
| Management of change | procedural | A change introducing a hazard nobody reviewed | Change screening; review by the right disciplines; pre-start-up check; closing out temporary changes |
| Operating procedures and shift handover | procedural | Steps omitted or conditions not passed on | Procedures kept current; handover record of open permits, bypasses and abnormal states |
| Competence of operators and supervisors | behavioural | Wrong or late action in an abnormal state | Training on upsets, drills on simulators or scenarios, assessment records |

## 2. Mitigation barriers

| Barrier | Type | What it limits | Controls that keep it healthy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gas and fire detection | active | Time between release and response | Coverage mapping; calibration and test; alarm routing and response |
| Emergency shutdown and isolation valves | active | Inventory that can be released | Valve function tests; closure time checks; fire-safe rating where required |
| Secondary containment (bunds, dykes, drains) | passive | Spread of a liquid release | Drain valve control; capacity; kept clear of other use |
| Fire protection (fire-water, foam, deluge) | active | Fire growth and exposure of equipment | Pump start tests; ring-main flow tests; foam stock; coverage after plant changes |
| Layout and separation | passive | People and equipment exposed | Occupied-building siting; control of temporary buildings; restricted areas |
| Emergency response and mutual aid | procedural | Duration and spread of the event | Plan matched to credible scenarios; drills with all roles; command arrangements; shared resources agreed |
| Evacuation and muster | procedural | People exposed | Alarm audibility; routes kept clear; head counts; refuge conditions |

## 3. Escalation factors that recur

Conditions that weaken a barrier. Treat each as a question to test against the export, never as a fact about it.
- A safety function bypassed or overridden and not tracked to restoration.
- Deferred inspection or maintenance on a critical item.
- Alarm counts too high for the operator to pick out the one that matters.
- A temporary change running longer than its approval.
- A permit written for the task but not for the neighbouring work.
- A turnaround or shutdown compressing planning time and adding unfamiliar contractors.
- Drawings or procedures that no longer match the plant.

## 4. Controls that support several barriers

Calibration and proof testing, defect and recommendation tracking, document control, competence assurance and shift handover recur under many barriers. A weakness in one of these is a systemic learning: count it once as a learning and list the barriers it touches.
