Bottom line

  • The prevention barriers at the seal were missing rather than weak: no specification for the part, no test after the repair, no independent check of the job and no review of repeat failures. Evidence: corroborated
  • On the mitigation side, detection, isolation and communication all acted, but too late: the release was seen at 02:15, the alarm sounded at 02:20 and the first valve was closed at 03:01. Evidence: corroborated
  • The decisive mitigation gaps were an isolation that needed people at the valve and a plan that gave no one the criteria or the authority to order it. Evidence: corroborated
  • Fire water worked on the night, carried by the electric pumps, while three of four diesel pumps were unavailable or unreliable. Evidence: direct
  • Eleven of the twelve fixes are above the administrative tier (two substitution, nine engineering); the one administrative fix, the plan's isolation criteria, is paired with remote isolation. Evidence: inferred

Prevention

design and specification (substitution of parts)

Control: substitutionType: proceduralState: absentOwner: Mechanical Maintenance (F02, T3)

Failure mode

The original gasket specification for the 1986 meter was not on file and no role owned it, so locally bought O-rings with no specification or quality check were the regular substitute (L4).

Fix

Re-establish the seal design (manufacturer data or an engineered equivalent), stock only certified parts against it and quarantine non-conforming seals.

Leading indicator

Share of critical pressure-boundary spares with a specification and certificate on file.

Evidence: corroborated
+28 supporting

maintenance and testing (testing after maintenance)

Control: engineeringType: proceduralState: absentOwner: Mechanical Maintenance (F02, T3)

Failure mode

No procedure required a test to design pressure before a high-pressure meter returned to service; the replaced joint was pressurised to operating pressure only (L4, L3).

Fix

Configure the work-order system so a pressure-boundary job cannot be closed, or the equipment released to operations, until a test result and witness are entered.

Leading indicator

Share of pressure-boundary jobs closed with a recorded test result and witness; attempted closures blocked for missing test data.

Evidence: direct
+21 supporting

competence and staffing (independent verification)

Control: engineeringType: proceduralState: absentOwner: Work Management & Planning (F08, T3)

Failure mode

The seal job of 18 September 2020 was manned with no independent quality-control role, test witness or closing supervisor, and no job safety analysis (L4, L3).

Fix

Add a safety-critical category to work planning that automatically adds inspection, test-witness and close-out steps signed by someone other than the performer.

Leading indicator

Share of safety-critical jobs with every verification step signed by someone other than the performer.

Evidence: corroborated

inspection and integrity (repeat failures on ageing equipment)

Control: engineeringType: proceduralState: absentOwner: Mechanical Maintenance (F02, T3)

Failure mode

Repeat seal failures on the same meter were treated as routine repairs; no reliability role, root-cause review or repair-or-replace decision existed (L4, L3).

Fix

Set an automatic repeat-failure trigger in the maintenance system that opens a root-cause task, and record a repair-or-replace decision for each repeat item.

Leading indicator

Repeat-failure items flagged per quarter and the share with a completed root-cause analysis.

Evidence: direct

Control: engineeringType: proceduralState: defeatedOwner: Engineering, Design, Documents & MOC (F07, T3)

Failure mode

The MOC procedure was published in August 2016 but not used after 2018, so the seal substitution never reached an engineering review (L4).

Fix

Build an MOC trigger into the work-order and stores issue screens: a non-catalogue or substitute part needs an MOC reference.

Leading indicator

MOCs raised per month, and the share of substitute-part issues with an MOC reference.

Mitigation

ignition control (hazardous-area equipment)

Control: substitutionType: passiveState: degradedOwner: Electrical Maintenance (F03, T3)

Failure mode

A flame-proof lamp in the hazardous area had three bolts missing and its glass case open, under a breakdown-led electrical maintenance practice; the findings name it as the probable ignition source (L4, L3).

Fix

Replace the legacy flame-proof lamps in zoned areas with certified, lower-surface-temperature fittings, inspected under a risk-based programme.

Leading indicator

Share of zoned-area light fittings replaced with certified fittings; inspection completion against plan.

Evidence: direct

Failure mode

Gas detection lit an annunciator but started no shutdown or suppression, and it alarmed about 5 minutes after security staff saw the cloud (L4, L2).

Add confirmed-gas voting logic that starts emergency shutdown and isolation automatically in the metering and gas terminal areas.

isolation and shutdown (remote isolation)

Control: engineeringType: activeState: worked partlyOwner: Operations & Control Room (F01, T3)

Failure mode

The emergency isolation valves could be closed only by hand at the valve, inside the area the vapour made unreachable; the first was closed 46 minutes after the leak was first detected (L4, L2).

Fix

Convert the gas terminal emergency isolation valves to remotely operated shutdown valves, operable from the control room and a safe second location.

Leading indicator

Share of identified emergency isolation points with remote operation; shutdown valve proof-test pass rate.

Evidence: corroborated
+13 supporting

emergency command and decision authority

Control: administrativeType: proceduralState: absentOwner: Emergency Planning & Command (F09b, T3)

Failure mode

The on-site disaster management plan named hydrocarbon leakage but gave no isolation or stoppage criteria and no delegated authority, so the decision travelled up the night-shift chain (L4).

Fix

Write isolation and stoppage criteria, valve lists and delegated authority down to control-room shift level into the plan, paired with the remote isolation above so the decision can be carried out from a safe place.

Leading indicator

Share of release scenarios in the plan with written criteria, valves and authority; time to isolate in night-shift drills.

Evidence: corroborated

communication and escalation

Control: engineeringType: proceduralState: worked partlyOwner: Operations & Control Room (F01, T2)

Failure mode

Security staff saw the release first, but their reports went through the CCTV control room and reached the gas terminal control room as 'some vapour cloud or steam leakage' (L4, L2).

Fix

Install a direct line from the perimeter posts and the CCTV room to each process control room, with a manual call point at the watch towers.

Time from first detection to control-room notification in joint drills.

fire-water and fire protection

Control: engineeringType: activeState: workedOwner: Fire-fighting Operations & Fire-water Systems (F09a, T3)

Failure mode

Tank level and fire-water header pressure stayed healthy and the electric pumps carried the fire cooling. Latent: three of four diesel pumps were under maintenance or tripping, with the cause not recorded (L4).

Fix

Diagnose the tripping fault, restore the diesel pumps, and hold them to a performance standard with regular run tests and a maximum allowed outage.

Leading indicator

Diesel fire-pump availability and test pass rate.

Summary

3

The weight sat on the prevention side. Five of the six prevention barriers were absent or bypassed at the seal: the specification, the test after maintenance, the independent check, the repeat-failure review and change management [; ; ; ; ]. Any one of them could have stopped an unspecified, untested joint going back into service.

Evidence: inferred

On the mitigation side, detection, isolation and communication acted only late or in part, the isolation criteria were absent and the ignition barrier was degraded. Detection informed but did not act [], isolation needed people at the valve [], and the plan gave no isolation criteria []. A damaged flame-proof lamp was the probable ignition source []. Fire water was the one barrier that worked, with a latent weakness in the diesel pumps [].

Evidence: corroborated

The fixes are strong: two substitution and nine engineering, and one administrative change that is itself the system fix for the plan. In the register, training appears only paired with another change (LR-06, LR-07, LR-08, LR-12), never alone [; ].

Evidence: inferred
