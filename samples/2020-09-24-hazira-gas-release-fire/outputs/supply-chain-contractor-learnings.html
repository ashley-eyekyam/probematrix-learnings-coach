Bottom line

  • A seal with no specification and no quality check was bought, issued and fitted as regular practice; it is the probable source of the release.
  • No role owned the specification of the legacy part, or the approval of a substitute.
  • The substitution never went through change review: a part chosen at the stores counter is a change.
  • A vendor-prepared drawing was used without the operator's check, and it did not match the field.

Learnings

6

Procurement and stores

If stores can buy and issue a pressure-boundary seal with no specification and no quality check, the substitute becomes the normal part. Owner: Materials management function

Locally purchased O-rings were bought and issued as regular practice in place of the original rectangular-slot gasket, with no specification or quality control at purchase. Nothing in the purchasing or issue step asked for a specification reference.

Evidence: direct
+10 supporting

If no role owns the specification of a legacy part, nobody re-establishes it when it is lost, and buying fills the gap by habit. Owner: Plant leadership, with the materials management and engineering functions

No accountable owner or recorded authority existed for the gasket's material specification and its procurement, and no substitute specification or quality-control information reached the maintainers.

Evidence: direct
+10 supporting

Engineering at the supply interface

A substitute part chosen at the stores counter is a change, and it needs the change review even when it has been used before. Owner: Engineering function (owner of management of change)

The decision to use locally procured O-rings was never recorded or reviewed under management of change, and no change had gone through that procedure since 2018. People did not see a like-for-unlike replacement as a change.

Evidence: direct

Vendors and OEMs

When the original maker's parts stop being bought for ageing equipment, the specification and the support go with them unless someone asks for them. Owner: Materials management function, with the engineering function

The meter dated from 1986, its gaskets were no longer procured from the original equipment maker, and the original specification was not available. The findings record no request to the maker or another supplier for the specification.

Evidence: direct
+10 supporting

A drawing prepared by a vendor is not a controlled document until the operator has checked and approved it. Owner: Engineering document control

After the original metering drawings were lost, a vendor prepared new ones. They were not checked or approved, and the valve and meter configuration on them did not match the field.

Evidence: direct
+3 supporting

Contractors and outsourced roles

Work handed to a drawing contractor needs the same acceptance check as work done in-house; the contractor's drawing entered use because the host's acceptance step was missing. Owner: Engineering document control

The replacement as-built drawing was prepared outside the organisation and not verified before it took the place of the original. The gap sits in the host's document control, not in the contractor.

Evidence: direct
+4 supporting

Recommendations and indicators

6

Make the stores system refuse to issue a pressure-boundary seal without a specification reference and an approved-substitute record.

Control: engineeringBarrier: Procurement control of pressure-boundary parts

Leading indicatorPressure-part issues blocked or referred for engineering approval each quarter, and their closure time

+6 supporting

Re-establish the seal design for the meter flange from the maker's data or an engineered equivalent, stock only certified parts against that specification, and quarantine non-conforming seals.

Control: substitutionBarrier: Pressure-boundary integrity (correct seal)

Leading indicator% of critical pressure-boundary spares with a specification and certificate on file

+6 supporting

Survey legacy equipment for lost specifications, and plan replacement of items that can no longer be maintained to specification.

Control: eliminationBarrier: Equipment obsolescence management

Leading indicator% of legacy items surveyed, and the number with a replacement or re-specification decision recorded

+6 supporting

Build a change-review trigger into the stores issue and work-order screens: a non-catalogue or substitute part needs a management-of-change reference before issue.

Control: engineeringBarrier: Management of change

Leading indicator% of substitute-part issues with a management-of-change reference, and change reviews raised per month

Give one named function ownership of pressure-part specifications and of approving any substitute, with quality checks at receipt for those parts.

Control: administrativeBarrier: Procurement control of pressure-boundary parts

Leading indicator% of pressure-boundary part receipts with a recorded quality check against the specification

Why not stronger

The stores interlock and the re-specification above are the stronger controls; they need an owner to keep the specifications they enforce up to date.

+6 supporting

Language check JC6 accepted: Assigning the owner is the organisational fix for an L4 ownership gap and pairs with the engineering and substitution changes in this section.

Require operator checking and approval of every vendor or contractor drawing before it becomes a controlled document, starting with a field check of the metering and emergency isolation drawings.

Control: administrativeBarrier: Controlled as-built drawings

Leading indicator% of vendor drawings in use with a recorded operator approval

Why not stronger

Drawing control is a records process; no engineering control replaces the check of a drawing against the field.

+4 supporting

Language check JC6 accepted: Approval of vendor drawings is the document-control system fix for this L3/L4 gap; no stronger tier applies to a records process.
