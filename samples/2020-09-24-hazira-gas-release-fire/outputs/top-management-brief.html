Bottom line

  • Specifications, change control and emergency isolation rules existed only on paper or not at all for legacy equipment; any other site with equipment of the same vintage may carry the same gaps. Evidence: corroborated
  • Protective systems were designed around people reaching the hazard: detection that only displays an alarm and isolation valves that can only be closed by hand at the valve. Evidence: corroborated

Executive summary

3

On 24 September 2020 condensate vapour escaped from a seal on a 1986 metering skid at the gas terminal. The cloud spread for about 46 minutes before the first isolation valve was closed by hand, and it ignited at 03:08:15 from a damaged flameproof light fitting [; ; ; ].

The analysis uses the four HFACS levels: unsafe acts (what people did at the point of work), preconditions (the conditions around them), supervision (how work was planned, checked and corrected) and organisational influences (resources, policy, process and culture). This brief draws on the organisational level only, because that is where enterprise decisions act [; ].

The committee is asked to decide on four enterprise standards (pressure-part specifications, change control in use, emergency isolation, and fire and gas executive action) and to note two matters within function authority (hazardous-area electrical maintenance and audit quality); they are set out under 'Decisions for top management' [; ; ; ; ].

Policies, standards and legal requirements implicated

6

Spares and specification control

The original gasket specification for the 1986 meter was not on file and no role owned it, so locally bought O-rings were used as regular practice with no specification or quality check at purchase. The spares controls in place did not cover this legacy pressure-boundary part [; ; ].

Evidence: direct
+8 supporting

Management of change

The management-of-change procedure was published in August 2016, but no change had gone through it since 2018, including the switch to a different seal. The procedure existed but was not applied [; ].

Evidence: direct

Emergency planning (PNGRB ERDMP Regulations)

The site emergency plan named the major hydrocarbon leak as a scenario but gave no criteria for isolation or stoppage and no authority below the plant manager to order it. What the evidence shows is a gap between the scenario and the decision; whether the plan meets the regulations is for the competent authority [; ; ; ].

Clauses: IN-ERDMP Evidence: direct
+5 supporting

Maintenance strategy for hazardous areas

Electrical maintenance of flameproof fittings in the Zone 1 area was breakdown-led, and a fitting with three missing bolts stayed in service until it became the probable ignition source [; ; ].

Evidence: direct
+3 supporting

Competence

No qualification for control-room staff was defined in the HR or operations policy; new staff received a four-day general course and no simulator practice before emergency duties [; ; ].

Clauses: ISO45001-7.2 Evidence: direct

Audit and corrective action (OISD standards; ISO 45001 9.2 and 10.2)

The self-safety audit checklist was not specific to the plant and recorded compliance where data was later found incomplete and not in line with OISD standards. Twelve external-audit recommendations from 2018 were extended to April 2021 without the approval the procedure required [; ; ; ].

Clauses: ISO45001-9.2, ISO45001-10.2, IN-OISD Evidence: direct

Decisions for top management

12

For decision: pressure-part specifications

Set one enterprise rule that no critical pressure-boundary part is fitted without a specification and an approved-substitute record, and that one function owns those specifications

Options
  • Mandatory enterprise standard with a stores block on issue
  • Guidance to sites, checked by audit
Trade-offs

A stores block stops unspecified parts at the point of issue but needs specifications re-established first for legacy equipment; guidance is quicker to issue and relies on the audit process this event showed did not detect the gap.

Critical spares with a specification

Measure

Share of critical pressure-boundary spares with a specification and certificate on file, by site

Deterioration looks like

Any site below the previous quarter, or any part issued without a specification

Owner

Engineering function

For decision: change control in use

Require each site to show that its management-of-change procedure is in use, not only published

Options
  • Quarterly MOC activity report with a sampled field check
  • Annual audit of MOC records
Trade-offs

A quarterly report with field sampling finds silent procedures within months but adds reporting load; an annual audit adds less load and leaves a gap of up to a year.

MOC activity

Measure

MOC records opened and closed per site per quarter, and field changes found without a record

Deterioration looks like

A quarter with no MOC records at an operating site, or any unrecorded change found

Owner

Engineering function

For decision: emergency isolation

Set an enterprise standard for emergency isolation: written isolation criteria in every emergency plan, isolation authority delegated to the shift, and remote operation for emergency isolation valves that need access to the hazard area

Options
  • Criteria and delegation first, remote operation by a capital programme
  • All three together at the highest-risk sites
Trade-offs

Criteria and delegation are written changes; remote operation is an engineering change that is likely to need capital and a shutdown window, but of the two it is the one that removes the need to walk into the release.

Time to isolation in drills

Measure

Time from a confirmed release to first isolation in drills, by site, and the share of emergency isolation valves operable remotely

Deterioration looks like

A drill in which isolation waits for authority above the shift, or no change in the remote share over two reporting periods

Owner

Operations function

For decision: fire and gas executive action

Decide whether gas detection at major-hazard sites must trigger automatic shutdown or protection, rather than only display an alarm

Options
  • Executive action at all major-hazard sites
  • Executive action where a risk study shows manual response is too slow
Trade-offs

Executive action removes reliance on a manual chain but brings spurious-trip risk and engineering cost; a risk-based approach targets cost but needs a study at each site first.

Detection systems with executive action

Measure

Share of fire and gas systems at major-hazard sites with defined executive actions, tested in the last test cycle

Deterioration looks like

Any system found display-only with no recorded risk decision

Engineering function

For noting: hazardous-area electrical maintenance

Electrical maintenance of flameproof fittings in hazardous areas moves from breakdown repair to planned inspection, within the maintenance function's authority

Options
  • Planned inspection with recorded condition of each fitting
Trade-offs

Planned inspection needs crew time and access; the evidence shows breakdown repair left a compromised fitting in service.

Maintenance function

For noting: audit quality

Audit checklists are written for each plant and filled checklists are retained, within the HSE function's authority

Options
  • Plant-specific checklists with retained records
Trade-offs

Plant-specific checklists take longer to write and audit; generic checklists here recorded compliance without plant data.
