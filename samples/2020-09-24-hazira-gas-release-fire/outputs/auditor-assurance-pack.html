Bottom line

  • Test returns to service first: pressure-boundary work went back into service tested only at operating pressure, with no procedure requiring more.
  • Test spares control: a seal with a lost specification was replaced by locally bought parts as normal practice, with no function owning the specification.
  • Test emergency isolation on paper and in the field: the plan gave no isolation criteria or delegated authority, and the valves could be closed only at the valve.
  • Test what detection does: fire and gas detection only lit an annunciator; it started no shutdown or suppression.
  • Test hazardous-area electrical inspection fitting by fitting: a damaged flameproof fitting stayed in service under breakdown-led maintenance.
  • Test the audits themselves: generic checklists recorded compliance without site data, and audit recommendations were extended without the required approval.

Why existing assurance did not catch it

4

Self-audits used a location checklist that was not specific to each plant and recorded compliance on most items, but the data behind the entries was incomplete; the internal audits gave observations without the filled checklists, so nobody could check what had been looked at.

Recommendations from the external safety audit were extended without the approval the procedure requires, so overdue safety actions had no governance step that forced a decision.

The records an audit would sample did not show the conditions: the malfunction notification for the meter was left open after the work and the signed calibration record did not show a setting change; the committee's report adds that electrical maintenance records listed only repaired items, not the inspection of the rest (OBS-27).

Drill records held no observations or actions, so drills could not reveal readiness gaps such as the missing isolation criteria.

Maintenance and testing

Pressure-boundary work is proved by a test to design pressure, under a written return-to-service procedure, before the equipment goes back into service.

What to sample

The last ten returns to service after work on a flange, seal or gasket in high-pressure hydrocarbon service.

Evidence of conformity

For each job: the procedure in force, a test record stating the test pressure against the design pressure, and the signature of the person accepting the equipment back.

Red flags seen in this investigation
  • Test recorded only as 'pressurised to operating pressure'
  • No return-to-service procedure for the equipment type
  • Test record dated after the equipment went back online
