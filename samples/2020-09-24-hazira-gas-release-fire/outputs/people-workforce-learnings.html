Bottom line

  • No qualification was defined for the control-room role, so people held emergency duty after a four-day general course.
  • Rostering had no rule that each shift include a person with practised emergency-isolation competence.
  • A pressure-boundary repair was manned like routine work: no inspector, no test witness and no close-out reviewer.
  • The authority to isolate in an emergency sat with senior roles, not with the role that saw the alarm first.
  • Drill records held no observations, so they could not show whether emergency competence was improving.

People-system learnings

6

Qualification and authorisation

A safety-critical role needs a defined qualification before anyone is assigned to it. Owner: HR and operations policy owners

The enquiry records that the qualification of control-room staff was not defined in HR or operations policy, that new and transferred staff received a four-day general course, and that most staff had attended one basic course since 2018 []. With no qualification to meet, there was nothing to check before a person took emergency duty. The committee's own benchmark was at least one month of training, preferably on simulators, before control-room duties [].

Evidence: direct
+1 supporting

Selection and placement

Recruitment and transfer into the control room had no role-specific standard, so placement rested on availability. Owner: HR business partner for operations

The same four-day general programme served new recruits and transferred staff alike, and the policy set no qualification for the post []. The findings read this as a selection-and-placement gap, not only a training gap: the night-shift posts were filled from the same broadly trained population []. The programme risk of staffing a major-hazard control room this way was not assessed.

Evidence: corroborated

Oversight of people standards

A known competence gap stays open when no one owns the standard and checks it before rostering. Owner: Competence-framework owner

The training posture for control-room staff was known and accepted: the findings place the gap with operations management and the policy that allowed it []. No rule tied assignment to emergency duty to a passed assessment, so the gap was carried from 2018 to the night of the release.

Evidence: direct
+2 supporting

Qualification and authorisation

Role descriptions must say who may isolate in an emergency, or the decision climbs the hierarchy while the release grows. Owner: Workforce planning with operations leadership

The disaster management plan gave no isolation criteria, so the authority to order isolation effectively sat with the most senior shift roles []. The control-room engineers telephoned the shift in-charges instead of isolating [], and the first isolating valve was closed after the senior officers arrived [].

Evidence: corroborated

Authorising a crew for safety-critical maintenance needs a task-level competence standard, not only a job title. Owner: Maintenance competence-framework owner

Crews were authorised for pressure-boundary work on the meter without the OEM specification or a test to design pressure [] [], and a calibration crew for the same meter worked without a checklist and left a range change out of the signed record []. No record shows who authorised the switch to locally bought seals [].

Evidence: direct

Oversight of people standards

Drill records that hold only dates cannot serve as evidence that emergency competence is being kept up. Owner: Competence-framework owner with HSE

Fire drill reports in SAP held no actions or observations, and a separate dataset was kept outside the approved guidelines []. A competence framework that relies on drills for refresher evidence needs the drill record to capture timings, observations and actions.

Evidence: direct

Rostering, manning and workload

3

Crew and team composition

A roster needs a rule that every shift includes at least one person qualified for the emergency the post may face. Owner: Rostering owner for operations

The night-shift control room was held by two engineers drawn from the same four-day-trained population, so neither could compensate for the other [] []. Both went out to find the leak and were driven back by the cloud [].

Evidence: corroborated
+2 supporting

Safety-critical maintenance needs to be manned with an independent checker, a test witness and a close-out reviewer, not only the person doing the work. Owner: Workforce planner for maintenance

The 18 September 2020 gasket replacement was resourced with no quality check of the part [], no one to perform or witness a design-pressure test [], and no one to close the notification []. The only control left was the executing crew's own knowledge.

Evidence: corroborated

Critical calibration work needs a second person in the team to verify and record every setting change. Owner: Workforce planner for instrumentation

During the December 2019 calibration of the meter, the range was changed from 16000 to 2540 mmwc and back with no mention in the signed records and no checklist []. No independent verifier was part of the team.

Evidence: direct

Hand-off to training design

1

For `training-designer`: the training needs this pack found are simulator or tabletop scenarios in which access to the source is denied and the crew must isolate from a distance [] []; the specification check and the test to design pressure for pressure-boundary work [] []; as-found and as-left recording in calibration []; and recording observations and actions after each drill []. This pack sets the standard and the staffing rule; the course content belongs there.

Limits of this pack

EVD-4052
document: On-site Disaster Management Plan (DMP) — hydrocarbon-leakage section omits isolation/stoppage decision criteria. Source records: "As per DMP On site Hazira Plant, guidelines to be followed in case of hydrocarbon leakage are included but does not include isolation/stoppage of facilities/pipelines" (p.32). Reinforced at p.27: "In ERDMP, scenario for Level 3 emergencies are taken from the plant failures. The DMP defines the scenario but not the decision: when does a hydrocarbon leak warrant unilateral pipeline isolation, and who has the authority to call it? This procedural gap directly correlates with the 46-minute response delay — personnel had no documented criterion to act on, and authority was concentrated above the shift-in-charge level (row 50). The committee's root cause #6 ("Non isolation/stoppage of 36 pipeline operation") flows from this finding.
