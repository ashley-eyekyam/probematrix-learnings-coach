Top clusters

F02 Mechanical Maintenance

Owns the release source: the meter seal and the work on it. The highest-rated findings say the original gasket specification was lost and never re-established, so locally bought O-rings with no specification or quality check became the regular substitute [; ], and that no procedure required a hydrostatic test before the meter went back into service [; ].

Evidence: corroborated

F01 Operations & Control Room

Owns the response once the release began. The highest-rated findings say the emergency isolation valves were local-manual only, with no remote operation from the control room [; ], and that the fire and gas detection system had no automatic shutdown or suppression interlock [; ]. Its lower-tier findings cover accepting the meter back into service without a test.

Evidence: corroborated

F07 Engineering, Design, Documents & MOC

Owns the design and change record. The highest-rated findings say the management-of-change procedure published in August 2016 had not been used since 2018, so the seal substitution was never reviewed [; ], and that the metering drawing was a vendor replacement, never approved, that did not match the field [; ].

Evidence: direct

F11 HSE, Process Safety & Audit

Owns the plans and the assurance around them. The highest-rated findings say the on-site disaster management plan gave no isolation or stoppage criteria for a major hydrocarbon leak [; ], and a breakdown-led electrical maintenance culture left a damaged flame-proof lamp in service [; ]. Its audit findings show generic checklists that could not find plant-specific defects [; ].

Evidence: direct

F05 Asset Integrity, Corrosion & Inspection

Owns the evidence that the equipment was deteriorating. The highest-rated findings say the meter kept failing at the seal and was left to routine resealing, with no root-cause or replacement programme [; ], and that pipe-wall loss found in 2017 had no corrective plan three years later [; ].

Evidence: direct
