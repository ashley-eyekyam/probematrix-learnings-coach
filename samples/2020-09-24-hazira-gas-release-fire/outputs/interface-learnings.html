F02 Mechanical Maintenance and F06 Materials Management

6

The seam

What passes between them is a part: the seal for the retaining plate of the 1986 condensate meter. Stores and procurement buy and issue it; maintenance fits it to a flange machined for a rectangular-slot gasket [; ; ].

Evidence: direct

What went wrong at the seam

An ownership gap. The original gasket specification was not on file and no role owned re-establishing it, so locally bought round O-rings were purchased with no specification or quality check and fitted as a regular practice [; ; ; ]. The switch was never recorded as a management-of-change decision [; ], and the job of 18 September 2020 was manned with no independent quality check of the part [; ].

Evidence: corroborated

Why neither side saw it

With no recorded authority and a flat authority gradient, staff at the working level made the substitution, and the practice was authorised without being reviewed against a specification [; ]. Each side could reasonably take the part as settled: stores supplied what the job used, and the job used what stores supplied. Three replacements in 22 months did not prompt a review [; ].

Evidence: inferred

Learning for each side

A pressure-boundary seal fitted without a written specification is an unreviewed change, however routine the substitute has become. Owner: Mechanical Maintenance (F02)

Maintenance fitted locally bought O-rings to a rectangular-slot flange across three replacements, with no specification, quality check or recorded authority [; ; ].

Evidence: corroborated

Buying a substitute for a pressure part without a specification moves an engineering decision to the purchase order. Owner: Materials Management (F06)

The acquisition process supplied an unspecified round O-ring for a flange machined for a rectangular-slot gasket, with no specification or quality control [; ; ].

Evidence: corroborated

Joint fix

Re-establish the meter seal specification (manufacturer data or an engineered equivalent), hold it as a controlled item, and have stores issue pressure-boundary seals only against that specification reference. A substitute part needs an approved change record before it is issued.

Control: substitutionBarrier: pressure-boundary integrity (correct seal)Owner: Mechanical Maintenance (F02)Co-owner: Materials Management (F06)

Leading indicatorShare of pressure-boundary seals issued from stores with a specification reference and a certificate, visible to both stores and maintenance.

Evidence: corroborated

F01 Operations & Control Room and F09b Emergency Planning & Command

6

The seam

What passes between them is a decision: whether, when and by whom the plant is isolated in a major release. Emergency planning writes it into the on-site disaster management plan; the control room and the shift in-charges have to take it on the night [; ; ; ].

Evidence: corroborated

What went wrong at the seam

Authority with neither side. The plan named hydrocarbon leakage but gave no isolation or stoppage criteria [; ], isolation authority was not allocated to the on-shift operators [; ], and the isolation intent was never passed to the night shift through the plan or standing orders []. The decision went up the chain by phone, and the first isolation valve was closed 46 minutes after the leak was first detected [; ; ].

Evidence: corroborated

Why neither side saw it

The major-leak scenario was formally identified in the plan, but its isolation criteria were never defined and the risk of leaving them out was never assessed [; ]. Each side could reasonably take the decision as covered: the plan named the scenario, and the shift followed the escalation chain it knew [; ].

Evidence: inferred

Learning for each side

An emergency plan that names a release scenario but not who may isolate, and on what criteria, leaves the decision to escalation. Owner: Emergency Planning & Command (F09b)

The plan's hydrocarbon-leak guidance omitted isolation and stoppage criteria and delegated authority [; ; ].

Evidence: direct

When a release blocks access to the source, the shift's task changes from finding the leak to isolating from a distance, and it needs the authority to do so at once. Owner: Operations & Control Room (F01)

Dense vapour stopped anyone approaching the source, while the isolation decision waited on senior staff who were called at about 02:35 [; ; ].

Evidence: corroborated

Joint fix

Write isolation and stoppage criteria, the isolation valve list and delegated authority down to control-room shift level into the plan, as one jointly owned section, and test it in unannounced night-shift drills without senior staff present.

Control: administrativeBarrier: emergency command and decision authorityOwner: Emergency Planning & Command (F09b)Co-owner: Operations & Control Room (F01)

Leading indicatorTime from confirmed release to the isolation decision in night-shift drills, recorded in the drill record both sides read.

Why not stronger

The stronger control, remote isolation of the gas terminal valves (LR-09), needs these criteria and this authority to be used without waiting for escalation; the two go together.

Evidence: corroborated

Language check JC6 accepted: the written criteria and delegated authority are the system fix for the plan (L4); the engineering control they pair with is the remote isolation of LR-09
