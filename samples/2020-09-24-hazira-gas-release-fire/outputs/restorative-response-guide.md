## Who is hurt?

**Q: How are the two control-room engineers who left the control room on foot towards the vapour cloud, and what happened to them that night that we do not yet know?** [FND-13708453; FND-BDD73BA2; EVD-1460; EVD-9220]
- Why it matters: They walked towards a dense cloud to find the leak and could not approach it [EVD-1460] [EVD-9220]; the export says nothing about how they are now.
- Put to: Site leader, with a peer supporter

## Whose obligation is it?

Meeting these needs is the organisation's obligation, not the obligation of the people involved. Site leadership owes them a hearing, plain information about the investigation, and support that does not depend on what the investigation finds. Line leaders owe them a conversation before any return to duty. HR and occupational health owe them access to support for as long as they need it.

The organisation also owes them the repair of the conditions they worked in. The findings show people on emergency duty with no defined qualification [EVD-5634], no standing authority below senior management to isolate [EVD-3932], and warnings that reached the control room late and softened [EVD-8871]. Those changes belong to the owners of the training policy, the emergency plan and the notification routes, and the people involved should hear what is being done about them. [FND-954553FD; FND-82761CC5; FND-2F736E5F; EVD-5634; EVD-3932; EVD-8871]

## The conditions they worked in

**Learning:** People held emergency duty in the control room with no defined qualification, after a four-day general course and no simulator practice. [FND-954553FD; FND-337CB67F; FND-0AEE9898; FND-8EC441F3; EVD-5634]

The enquiry records that control-room operations were carried out by people who had a four-day general training programme, with no defined qualification [EVD-5634]. The night-shift crew was given an isolation task its training had not prepared it for. This is a condition the organisation set, not a shortcoming of the people on shift.

*(evidence: direct; learning: LR-12)*

**Learning:** No role below senior management had standing authority to isolate, so the isolation decision travelled up the chain by telephone. [FND-82761CC5; FND-8DAFDADC; EVD-4052; EVD-3932]

The on-site emergency plan covered hydrocarbon leakage but not isolation or stoppage [EVD-4052], and the organogram shows no role between the Shift In-charge and the Plant Manager with standing authority to close the isolation valve [EVD-3932]. People on shift were left to seek permission for the one action that would stop the release.

*(evidence: direct; learning: LR-07)*

**Learning:** Nothing prompted the shift to switch from finding the leak to isolating from a distance once the cloud blocked access. [FND-36179046; FND-4245FBF2; EVD-9612; EVD-7340]

The shift leaders' statements describe the response staying on locating the leak while the decision to isolate was escalated [EVD-9612] [EVD-7340]. Normal leak response is to find and fix the source; the plan and the training gave no point at which to stop approaching.

*(evidence: corroborated; learning: LR-08)*

**Learning:** Warnings from the people who saw the release first reached the control room through relay points and in softer words. [FND-2F736E5F; FND-BC00FF3A; FND-37BC45CF; EVD-6234; EVD-8871; EVD-1460]

Perimeter reports went to the CCTV control room and the main gate rather than to the control room that could act [EVD-6234] [EVD-8871]. The CCTV operator verified heavy leakage, but the control room was told of some vapour cloud or steam leakage [EVD-8871] [EVD-1460].

*(evidence: corroborated; learning: LR-13)*

**Learning:** The new control room had blast-proof walls but ordinary doors, in an area that filled with vapour. [FND-D871D980; EVD-1460; EVD-9941]

The equipment references list the Gas Terminal control room as new and blast-proof, with doors that were not blast-proof [EVD-1460] [EVD-9941]. The people in it were sheltering behind protection that was incomplete where they came and went.

*(evidence: corroborated; learning: LR-25)*

## Support for the people involved

- [ ] Offer each person involved a conversation with a peer supporter before any further interview, and let them choose who it is.
- [ ] Treat the people whose acts appear in the findings as possible second victims: the control-room engineers, the shift leaders and the maintenance crew. Offer them the same support as anyone else hurt by the event. [FND-13708453; FND-82761CC5; FND-615DB819]
- [ ] Keep support apart from the investigation: what a person tells a peer supporter does not go into the investigation file, and say so at the start.
- [ ] Tell the people involved, before the findings are shared more widely, what the findings say about the conditions and what is being changed. *(before the findings are shared)* [FND-954553FD; FND-82761CC5; FND-2F736E5F]
- [ ] Offer access to employee assistance and occupational health, and make the offer again at each later check-in.
- [ ] Before anyone returns to the control room or the job, ask what would help, and walk the route with them if they want to. *(before return to duty)* [FND-D871D980]

## Limits of this pack

- The export holds nothing about the health, feelings or wishes of the people involved; every need in this guide is a question to ask them.
- The export's initial report states no injuries to plant personnel and on-shift contractors and says the status of the area outside the perimeter was being verified; it records no later update, so this guide cannot say whether anyone outside the plant was hurt.
- The analysis is a desk review of the published enquiry report; it had no access to the people involved, and it does not review any individual.
