Bottom line

  • The release came from a pressure-boundary seal fitted without a specification and put back into service without a test to design pressure: check whether your site carries the same two conditions.
  • Isolation took about 46 minutes, because the plan held no isolation criteria and the valves could be closed only by hand inside the vapour cloud: check your emergency plan and isolation valves next.

About this self-assessment

1

A self-assessment for each site. Results show coverage and conditions, never a ranking of sites.

How to use it

2

The HSE lead at each site works through every self-check with the people who do the work, against the site's own records and plant. Look for the condition, not for a repeat of the event: the same condition can sit behind a different leak, a different valve or a different fire.

Record one answer for each self-check on the site's return: present, partly, absent or n/a. Add the site's response and the role of the person who assessed it. The organisation sets the date for returns.

Self-checks

11
Condition to look for
A pressure-boundary seal or gasket is replaced with a part that has no written specification, or with a substitute no one has approved.
Learning
LR-01
Evidence to check
For each pressure-boundary seal or gasket fitted in the last two years, a specification or an approved substitute is on file and matches the part that was bought.
Where to look
Spares and purchase records, the stores catalogue and the maintenance history of older equipment
Condition to look for
Pressure-boundary work goes back into service without a test to design pressure, because no procedure requires one.
Learning
LR-02
Evidence to check
Recent seal, gasket or flange jobs on pressure equipment each have a test record that names the test type and pressure, and a return-to-service procedure requires it.
Where to look
Work orders, test certificates and the procedure index
Condition to look for
A repair on a high-consequence pressure boundary rests on one person's judgement, with no independent check of the part, the method or the test.
Learning
LR-03
Evidence to check
Job plans for these repairs name a check of the part, a witnessed test and a close-out review, and the records show each was done.
Where to look
Job plans, permits and inspection or witness-point records
Condition to look for
Repeat failures of the same component are repaired as routine each time, with no root-cause review or engineering look at why they recur.
Learning
LR-05
Evidence to check
The maintenance system can list the components repaired three or more times in two years, and each one has a root-cause review or an engineering decision on record.
Where to look
The failure history in the maintenance management system and the reliability or engineering review log
Condition to look for
The management-of-change procedure exists on paper, but a different part, material or pipe route goes in without it.
Learning
LR-06
Evidence to check
The MOC register shows recent entries, and a sample of recent part or material substitutions each has an MOC record.
Where to look
The MOC register, recent purchase substitutions and modification work orders
Condition to look for
The emergency plan names a major release but gives no criteria for when to isolate and no standing authority for the shift to order it.
Learning
LR-07
Evidence to check
For each major release scenario, the plan states the isolation trigger, the valves to close and which shift role may order isolation without waiting for a senior manager.
Where to look
The on-site emergency plan, its checklists and the control-room standing orders
Condition to look for
When a release blocks access, the response plan still sends people to find the leak instead of switching to isolation from a safe place.
Learning
LR-08
Evidence to check
Emergency drills and the control-room decision aids include a release that blocks access, and drill records show the switch to remote or upstream isolation.
Where to look
Drill reports, emergency scenario cards and the control-room console aids
Condition to look for
Emergency isolation valves can be closed only by hand at the valve, inside the area a release would make unreachable.
Learning
LR-09
Evidence to check
The list of emergency isolation valves shows for each whether it can be closed remotely, and the risk that a release denies access to it has been assessed.
Where to look
The isolation valve list, the P&IDs and the site's risk assessment for releases
Condition to look for
Gas detectors only raise an alarm: they start no shutdown, isolation or protection, and their readings are not kept.
Learning
LR-10
Evidence to check
The fire and gas cause-and-effect chart shows which detectors trip a shutdown or start protection, and the control system stores detector history.
Where to look
The fire and gas cause-and-effect chart, the control system configuration and the alarm history
Condition to look for
Damaged flameproof electrical equipment stays in service in a hazardous area, because electrical maintenance waits for breakdowns.
Learning
LR-11
Evidence to check
Each item of hazardous-area electrical equipment has its own inspection record within the inspection interval, and the defects found are closed out.
Where to look
The hazardous-area equipment register, the electrical inspection records and a walk of the hazardous areas
Condition to look for
Control-room staff hold emergency duties with no defined qualification and no simulator or practised emergency training.
Learning
LR-12
Evidence to check
A control-room qualification is defined, and each person on the emergency roster has met it, with simulator or drill practice on record.
Where to look
Training records, the competence framework and the control-room roster
