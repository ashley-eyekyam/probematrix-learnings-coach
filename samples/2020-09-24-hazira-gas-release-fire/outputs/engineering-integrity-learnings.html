Bottom line

  • The release came from a seal fitted with no specification and returned to service without a test to design pressure; neither condition had an engineering owner.
  • The management-of-change procedure existed but was not used, so the substitution had no review.
  • Detection only informed and the isolation valves were local-only: the design left the response to people who could not reach the valves.
  • The probable ignition source was a flameproof lamp with missing bolts, under breakdown-led electrical maintenance.
  • Integrity warnings and drawings were recorded but not acted on or approved; the records themselves need owners.

Specification and substitution of parts

If the specification for a pressure-boundary part is lost and no function owns re-establishing it, a substitute part becomes normal practice. Owner: Technical authority (engineering function)

The 1986 meter was built for a rectangular-slot gasket. Its original specification was not on file, and no material specification or procurement policy existed for these gaskets, so locally bought O-rings were fitted as regular practice. The condition persisted because no function owned the specification or the approval of a substitute.

Testing after maintenance

If no standard defines the test for a high-pressure joint before return to service, the quickest available check becomes the test, and a weak joint passes. Owner: Technical authority (engineering function)

After the gasket change on 18 Sep 2020 the system was only pressurised to operating pressure; no hydro test to design pressure was done, and there was no procedure for calibration or return to service of these high-pressure meters. The metering-skid workspace had no specifications, procedures or checklists.

Evidence: direct

Return-to-service pressure test

Control: administrativeType: preventionState: absentOwner: Technical authority (engineering function)

Failure mode

No procedure required a test to design pressure, so pressurising to operating pressure was taken as proof of the joint.

Fix

A return-to-service standard that sets the test, the test pressure and the witness, enforced in the work-order system.

Leading indicator

% of pressure-boundary jobs closed with a recorded test result and witness

Route: MOC. Configure the work-order system so a pressure-boundary job cannot be closed, or the equipment released, until a test result against the technical authority's return-to-service standard is entered.

Change management

A management-of-change procedure that is published but never triggered lets changes in parts and materials through without review. Owner: Technical authority (engineering function)

The procedure was published in August 2016, but no change had been taken through it since 2018. The switch from the maker's gasket to locally bought O-rings had no change review, and people did not recognise a like-for-unlike replacement as a change.

Emergency isolation design

Emergency isolation valves that can only be worked at the valve are out of reach in the event they exist for. Owner: Technical authority (engineering function)

The gas terminal emergency valves were supplied as local-manual valves with no remote operation from the control room, and were never upgraded. The vapour cloud kept people away, and the first valve was closed by hand once the shift in-charge reached it.

Hazardous-area equipment integrity

If electrical maintenance in hazardous areas answers breakdowns instead of inspecting explosion protection, defects that do not stop the equipment working stay in service. Owner: Electrical engineering function

A flameproof lamp at the skid had three bolts missing and its glass case open; the committee named it the probable ignition source. Electrical maintenance leaned towards breakdown work with little systematic inspection, and the post-incident audit grouped all nine lamps under one status. The ignition source is probable, not proven.

Inspection and corrosion programmes

If inspection results are recorded but nobody owns turning them into a decision, the organisation knows about degradation and keeps operating with it. Owner: Asset integrity function

Wall loss found in 2017 had no corrective plan three years later; pitting found in July 2019 on a zero-corrosion-allowance 36-inch line was treated by painting, with no fitness-for-service review; the four-year thickness schedule slipped. These lines did not cause this release: the learning is about how integrity warnings are handled.

Detection, alarm and shutdown design

If fire and gas detection is designed to inform rather than act, every protective action waits on people noticing, checking and deciding. Owner: Technical authority (engineering function)

The hydrocarbon and H2S detectors were configured to the annunciator panel only, with no history in the control system and no automatic shutdown or suppression. No policy required the interlock, and the system was never upgraded, so the first alarm came after people had already seen the cloud.

Process signals at the control-room display

If transmitters at a leak point send nothing to the control system and its clocks disagree, operators lose the pressure trend that could show a leak, and the sequence has to be rebuilt afterwards from other records. Owner: Instrumentation and control engineering

Three pressure transmitters on the metering lines were shown on the drawing but had no data in the control system, so operators had no pressure trend at the source. Clocks across the control rooms differed by 2 to 12 minutes, so times on the day had to be taken from the camera and witness statements.
