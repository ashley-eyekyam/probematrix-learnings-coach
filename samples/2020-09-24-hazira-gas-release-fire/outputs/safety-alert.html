What happened

1

On 24 September 2020 condensate vapour escaped from a seal on a 1986 metering skid at a gas terminal, six days after the seal had been replaced [; ]. The vapour cloud spread for about 46 minutes before the first isolation valve was closed by hand [; ]. It then ignited at a damaged flameproof light fitting and caused a major fire [; ].

Why it matters to you

1

All three learnings apply industry-wide. Any site with legacy pressure equipment, locally bought spares or maintenance run on a work notification alone can hold the same conditions today [; ; ].

What we learned

3

If the specification for a pressure-boundary part is lost and no role owns it, crews fit whatever part is to hand, and the substitute becomes normal practice.

Locally bought O-rings replaced a slot gasket with no specification, check or approval; the seal fitted on 18 September failed six days later.

Evidence: corroborated

If no procedure says how an opened high-pressure joint is tested before service, crews use the quickest check, and a weak joint passes.

The joint was pressurised only to operating pressure, with no test to design pressure, and went back online about 46 hours later.

Evidence: corroborated

If a job that can cause a major release is planned like routine work, the fitter's knowledge is the only barrier.

No one was assigned to check the part, the method or the test.

Evidence: corroborated

What to check now

4
Hold point Do not return an opened pressure-boundary joint to service until a recorded test to design pressure has passed.
List the pressure-boundary seals on legacy equipment that have no specification on file.
Ask stores whether a pressure-boundary seal can be issued without a specification reference.
Check whether safety-critical maintenance jobs carry an independent check of part, method and test.
