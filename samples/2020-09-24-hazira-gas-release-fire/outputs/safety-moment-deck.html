Bottom line

  • No specification, no fit: a seal that does not match the original is a change, not a spare.
  • A joint you opened is not proven until it has passed a recorded test to the written test pressure.
  • When the same fault comes back, say so on the job record; a repeat is a warning, not a routine job.

What happened

1

On 18 Sep 2020 the seal on a 1986 condensate meter at the gas terminal was changed. The maker's specification had been lost, so a locally bought round O-ring was fitted, as had become regular practice, and the system was brought only to operating pressure, with no test to design pressure [; ; ; ]. The meter went back on line the next day and was in use on 24 Sep, when that seal was the probable source of a release that later caught fire [; ].

Did you know?

3

The meter was built for a rectangular-slot gasket; the round O-ring was a local substitute, bought with no specification or quality check [; ].

This was the third seal change on the same meter in about 22 months, and a malfunction notification had been raised the day before the last one. None of the repeats led to a review of why it kept leaking [; ; ; ].

No written procedure covered testing these high-pressure meters before they went back into service, so holding at operating pressure became the normal check [; ].

What you can do

3
Check the part against the groove and the specification before you fit it. If nobody can show a specification, stop and ask the supervisor. before fitting a seal or gasket on a pressure part
Hold point Before you hand the equipment back, ask for the written test pressure and the witness, and write the test result on the job record; if no test was done, write that. before return to service
Write "repeat failure" on the job record when you fix the same fault again, and tell the supervisor how often you have seen it. when a fault comes back

Questions for the crew

3

Which pressure parts on our unit have no specification we can find?

Why it matters

The seal on this meter had no specification on file, so a substitute became the normal part [; ].

Answer

A good answer names the parts, where their specification should be kept, and who the crew asks when a spare differs from the original.

What test proves a joint we have just made, and where is the result written?

Why it matters

After the seal change the system was only brought to operating pressure, with no test to design pressure [; ].

Answer

A good answer gives the test, the test pressure, who witnesses it and where the result is recorded before operations accept the equipment.

Which items do we keep fixing, and who decides whether we repair again or look for the cause?

Why it matters

Repeat seal failures on this meter were handled as routine repairs [; ].

Answer

A good answer names the items, says where a repeat would show on the job record, and names the role that starts a cause review.

EVD-8645
document: Lost OEM gasket specification; market-sourced O-rings used as substitutes without QC. Source records: "The meter is very old (1986) and having rectangular slot for the gasket. Original Specifications of gasket are not available, and the gaskets were not being procured from the OEMs. Instead, use of locally purchased O ring was done in its place as a regular practice. There was no specification or quality control during the purchase of the O rings. There is no record of date or authority of the decision to switch over from OEM supplied gasket to locally purchased gasket" (p.31). Six omissions documented in two sentences: lost spec, no OEM procurement, market O-ring substitution, no specification, no quality control, no documented authorisation. The committee's root cause #2 ("Use of nonstandard O ring") and #3 ("Lack of control over material sourcing") flow directly from this finding. The same governance failure underlies the absence of MOC application across the plant since 2018 (row 47).

Source records: "After calibration of the meter 310FE01, the gasket was replaced on 18.09.2020. No hydro test was done after replacement of the gasket. The system was only pressurised to operating pressure (Both meters were in line) and isolated. Next day meter 310FE01 was taken on online and was in use at the time of Incident" (p.31). System pressurised only to operating pressure; standard pressure-boundary integrity test (hydro test to design pressure) not performed.
