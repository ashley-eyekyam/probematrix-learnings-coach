## Bottom line

- Real competence gaps: specifying and testing pressure parts, recording calibration changes, and switching from locating a leak to isolating it. [FND-54180594; FND-7C8ADD7C; FND-E4596837; EVD-8645; EVD-2506; EVD-5634]
- Most of what went wrong is a system problem training cannot fix: no specification, no return-to-service procedure, no remote isolation, no isolation authority in the plan. [FND-23139BF9; FND-257C1100; FND-31405D96; FND-82761CC5; EVD-2894; EVD-4052]
- Control-room staff held emergency duties after a four-day general course with no defined qualification or simulator practice; a qualification standard is needed before more courses. [FND-7885BCF5; EVD-5634] *(learning: LR-12)*
- Teach to the level of the cause: crew modules for acts and conditions, supervisor and manager modules for checks, warnings and authority. [FND-FA807C69; FND-D14B8424; EVD-0591; EVD-1494]

## Modules

### Module: Returning pressure equipment to service [FND-8C0BB623; FND-212DC9B7; FND-54180594; EVD-8645; EVD-2894]

- Objective: Given a pressure-boundary job, the learner names the part specification, the test, the test pressure and the witness, and stops the job when any of them is missing.
- Audience: Maintenance fitters and technicians
- Duration: Half a day

**Content:**
1. Case: a meter in service since 1986 was built for a rectangular-slot gasket; its specification was lost, and locally bought O-rings were fitted as regular practice with no specification or quality check.
2. Case: after the last seal change the system was only brought to operating pressure; there was no hydro test to design pressure, and the meter was in use when it failed.
3. Activity: sort sample seals into "matches the specification", "approved substitute" and "stop and ask", using the item's records.
4. Activity: walk a return-to-service record and find what is missing.
5. System note: this module works only with a written specification and a return-to-service procedure; where they are missing, the learner is taught to stop and ask, not to fill the gap.
6. Competence standard: to be set by the competence system owner
7. Refresher interval: to be set by the competence system owner

**Assessment:**
1. Practical: complete a return-to-service record for a simulated job, observed by an assessor.
2. Questions: assessment items on specification and testing.

*(+10 supporting; learning: LR-01)*

### Module: Responding to a release you cannot reach [FND-F887999D; FND-E4596837; FND-C75F81B9; EVD-1460; EVD-9612]

- Objective: In a simulated release that denies access, the learner reports "access denied", names the isolation points that can be reached safely and asks for the isolation decision without delay.
- Audience: Control-room engineers and operators, security staff
- Duration: Half a day, simulator or tabletop

**Content:**
1. Case: security staff saw the cloud first; the control room heard of it as "some vapour cloud or steam leakage"; engineers tried to approach and were driven back; the first valve was closed by hand at 03:01.
2. Case: the gas detectors only lit a panel and started no protective action.
3. Activity: on the area drawing, mark which valves isolate each section and where each is worked from.
4. Activity: practise the standard release message between a security post and the control room.
5. System note: this module supports, and does not replace, remote isolation, executive gas detection and written isolation authority, which are system changes for manager-coach.
6. Competence standard: to be set by the competence system owner
7. Refresher interval: to be set by the competence system owner

**Assessment:**
1. Simulator or tabletop scenario, scored on the time to the isolation request and the content of the release message.
2. Questions: assessment items on alarms and isolation.

*(+10 supporting; learning: LR-08)*

### Module: Calibration records that show what was done [FND-85748D86; FND-7C8ADD7C; FND-D58142C9; EVD-2506]

- Objective: The learner records every setting changed during a calibration, including changes reversed, with as-found and as-left values, and does not sign a record that leaves a change out.
- Audience: Instrument technicians
- Duration: Two hours

**Content:**
1. Case: during a calibration in December 2019 the meter's range setting was changed and later changed back, with neither change on the signed records and no checklist.
2. Activity: complete a calibration record from a worked example that includes a reversed change.
3. System note: a calibration checklist for critical meters is a system change; until it exists, the learner uses the worked example as the record.
4. Competence standard: to be set by the competence system owner
5. Refresher interval: to be set by the competence system owner

**Assessment:**
1. Practical: an assessor reviews a completed calibration record for a critical meter.

*(+10 supporting; learning: LR-16)*

### Module: Accepting work back: hold points, notifications and permits [FND-904FCA63; FND-FA807C69; FND-63B6D260; EVD-2894; EVD-0591; EVD-5105]

- Objective: The supervisor applies the return-to-service hold points: a recorded test with a witness, the part's specification reference, a closed notification and a closed permit, and declines to accept work that lacks them.
- Audience: Maintenance and operations supervisors
- Duration: Half a day

**Content:**
1. Case: the seal job ran on a maintenance notification with no job safety analysis; no one checked the part, the method or the test, and the notification was not closed or reviewed.
2. Activity: coaching conversation on a job handed back late in the shift without a test record.
3. Activity: map each hold point to the form where it should live (permit, return-to-service certificate, shift log or job card).
4. System note: supervisors can hold the line only if the work-order system and management back them; those changes are for manager-coach.
5. Competence standard: to be set by the competence system owner
6. Refresher interval: to be set by the competence system owner

**Assessment:**
1. Observed coaching conversation, assessed against the hold points.
2. Questions: assessment items on supervision and the levels of cause.

*(+10 supporting; learning: LR-02)*

### Module: Reading repeat failures and integrity warnings [FND-D14B8424; FND-256214D9; EVD-1494; EVD-5527]

- Objective: The manager recognises a repeat failure or an unactioned inspection result as a reliability warning and routes it to a root-cause review or a fitness-for-service decision.
- Audience: Maintenance, integrity and planning managers
- Duration: Two hours

**Content:**
1. Case: the same meter's gasket was replaced three times in about 22 months with the same substitute part, and none of the repairs triggered a root-cause review.
2. Case: pipe-wall loss found in 2017 had no corrective plan three years later, and pitting found in July 2019 on a zero-corrosion-allowance line was treated by painting.
3. Activity: from a maintenance history extract, find the bad actors and decide the route for each.
4. Competence standard: to be set by the competence system owner
5. Refresher interval: to be set by the competence system owner

**Assessment:**
1. Case review: the manager presents the route chosen for each warning and why.

*(+10 supporting; learning: LR-05)*

### Module: Isolation authority and the emergency plan [FND-4B285961; FND-82761CC5; FND-D1DF4895; EVD-5634; EVD-4052; EVD-3932]

- Objective: The senior shift leader states the isolation criteria, the valves and the delegated authority for each release scenario, and leads an isolation decision in a night-shift exercise without waiting for escalation.
- Audience: Shift in-charges and emergency-response leaders
- Duration: Half a day, tabletop

**Content:**
1. Case: the plan named the hydrocarbon-leak scenario but not isolation; no one below the plant manager held standing authority to close the emergency valves; escalation ran one call at a time.
2. Activity: tabletop exercise with injects from the night, run without senior staff present.
3. System note: the criteria and authority must first be written into the plan by its owner; until then this module surfaces the gap rather than teaching an authority that does not exist.
4. Competence standard: to be set by the competence system owner
5. Refresher interval: to be set by the competence system owner

**Assessment:**
1. Tabletop exercise, assessed on the time to the isolation decision and the use of the criteria.

## Scenario exercises

**Scenario:** Decision point 1: the original seal is not in stores and the meter is needed back. Decision point 2: the job is finished and nobody has written down a test pressure. Decision point 3: operations ask to put the meter back on line now. [FND-9A8F3D7A; FND-89962239; EVD-8645]

1. Facilitator note: let the group say what made the usual O-ring the sensible choice at the time before discussing alternatives.
2. Facilitator note: ask what needs to exist (a specification, a procedure, a named witness) for "stop" to be the easy answer.
3. Facilitator note: close on what the learner will ask for on the next job, not on blame.

*(learning: LR-01)*

**Scenario:** Decision point 1: a call reports "some vapour cloud" and the gas alarm sounds. Decision point 2: you go out and cannot see or approach the source. Decision point 3: the person who can authorise isolation is not on site. [FND-E4596837; FND-097AB964; EVD-9220; EVD-1460]

1. Facilitator note: pause at each decision point and ask what the learner knows at that moment, not what the investigation later found.
2. Facilitator note: draw out which valves could be reached safely and who could be asked.
3. Facilitator note: record the system changes the group names for the manager-coach list.

*(learning: LR-08)*

**Scenario:** Decision point 1: mid-calibration, a range setting has to be changed to complete a check. Decision point 2: the setting is put back. Decision point 3: the record is handed over for signature. [FND-0BA85877; FND-8D744668; EVD-2506]

1. Facilitator note: ask where the reversed change would be recorded today.
2. Facilitator note: ask who would notice if it were not recorded, and when.

## Assessment

**Q: The seal in stores does not match the shape of the groove, and nobody can find the specification. What do you do?** [FND-8C0BB623; EVD-8645]
- Options:
  - Fit it: it is what the crew normally uses
  - Stop and ask the supervisor for the specification or an approved substitute **(correct)**
  - Fit it and record it as a temporary repair
  - Fit it and test at operating pressure
- Why it matters: Recognising a non-specified pressure part and stopping.
- Answer: Stop and ask the supervisor for the specification or an approved substitute; do not fit the part on the strength of "what we normally use".
- Good evidence looks like: The learner names who they would ask and where the specification should be recorded.

*(learning: LR-01)*

**Q: Why does pressurising a repaired joint to operating pressure not prove it?** [FND-212DC9B7; EVD-2894]
- Why it matters: Understanding the purpose of a test to design pressure.
- Answer: Operating pressure does not prove the joint against the pressures it must withstand; a written test to the test pressure, with a witness, is what proves it.
- Good evidence looks like: The learner names the test, the test pressure source and the witness.

*(learning: LR-02)*

**Q: What three things must be on the record before pressure-boundary equipment returns to service?** [FND-904FCA63; EVD-2894]
- Options:
  - The part's specification reference **(correct)**
  - A recorded test to the written test pressure **(correct)**
  - The witness's signature **(correct)**
  - The crew's shift roster
  - A note that the joint held at operating pressure
- Why it matters: Knowing the return-to-service hold points.
- Answer: The part's specification reference, a recorded test to the written test pressure, and the witness's signature.
- Good evidence looks like: All three named without prompting.

*(learning: LR-02)*

**Q: You are about to replace the same gasket on the same meter for the third time. What should happen besides the repair?** [FND-69067A1C; EVD-1494]
- Why it matters: Recognising a repeat failure as a reliability warning.
- Answer: Record it as a repeat failure and raise a root-cause request, so the item is reviewed rather than repaired again unseen.
- Good evidence looks like: The learner names where the repeat is recorded and who receives the request.

*(learning: LR-05)*

**Q: During calibration you change a range setting and later change it back. What goes on the record?** [FND-85748D86; EVD-2506]
- Why it matters: Keeping a complete calibration record.
- Answer: Both changes, with as-found and as-left values; the signed record must show what was done, not only the end state.
- Good evidence looks like: A completed example record that includes the reversed change.

*(learning: LR-16)*

**Q: On this site, what did a gas alarm on the annunciator panel do by itself?** [FND-A245072B; EVD-8657]
- Options:
  - It shut down the plant
  - It started fire suppression
  - It only displayed the alarm; every protective action depended on people **(correct)**
  - It closed the isolation valves
- Why it matters: Knowing what the alarm does and does not do.
- Answer: It only displayed the alarm; it did not shut down the plant or start fire suppression, so every protective action depended on people.
- Good evidence looks like: The learner can say which alarms on their unit trip equipment and which do not.

*(learning: LR-10)*

**Q: You go out to find a leak and the cloud stops you approaching. What is your next step?** [FND-F887999D; EVD-9612]
- Options:
  - Keep looking for another way to the source
  - Stop approaching, tell the control room "access denied" and ask for isolation from points that can be reached safely **(correct)**
  - Wait at the edge of the cloud for the shift in-charge
  - Go back to the control room and watch the alarm panel
- Why it matters: Switching from locating to isolating.
- Answer: Stop approaching, tell the control room "access denied", and ask for isolation from the points that can be reached safely.
- Good evidence looks like: The learner names the isolation points for their area and where they are worked from.

*(learning: LR-08)*

**Q: You are on a security post and see a white cloud near a process area. Whom do you call first, and what do you say?** [FND-C75F81B9; EVD-1460]
- Why it matters: Reporting a release so the control room can act.
- Answer: The control room for that area, saying "hydrocarbon release", where it is and how big it is.
- Good evidence looks like: The learner names the right control room for each area they watch.

*(learning: LR-13)*

**Q: True or false: a flameproof lamp that still lights is still flameproof, even with three bolts missing from its cover.** [FND-784A23E4; EVD-6916]
- Options:
  - True
  - False **(correct)**
- Why it matters: Recognising a defect in explosion-protected equipment.
- Answer: No. A flameproof fitting with missing bolts or an open cover is not flameproof; report it and have it made safe the same shift.
- Good evidence looks like: The learner lists what to check on a flameproof fitting on rounds.

*(learning: LR-11)*

**Q: Level of cause: "Locally bought O-rings were fitted as regular practice because the original specification had been lost and no one owned re-establishing it." At which level does the main fix sit, and why?** [FND-23139BF9; EVD-8645]
- Options:
  - Level 1, unsafe acts
  - Level 2, preconditions
  - Level 3, supervision
  - Level 4, organisational influences **(correct)**
- Why it matters: Placing a cause at its level (L1 to L4).
- Answer: Level 4, organisational: the missing specification and owner are resource and policy conditions. The fix is an owner and a specification, not an instruction to the fitter.
- Good evidence looks like: The learner separates the act (L1) from the organisational condition (L4).

*(learning: LR-01)*

**Q: Level of cause: "The maintenance notification was not closed or reviewed after the job." Which level is this, and what kind of fix does it need?** [FND-FA807C69; EVD-0591]
- Options:
  - Level 1, unsafe acts
  - Level 2, preconditions
  - Level 3, supervision **(correct)**
  - Level 4, organisational influences
- Why it matters: Placing a cause at its level (L1 to L4).
- Answer: Level 3, supervision: a check and correction that did not happen. The fix is a change to checks and oversight, such as a close-out review, not a reminder to the crew.
- Good evidence looks like: The learner names a supervision-level fix.

*(learning: LR-04)*

**Q: Level of cause: "The vapour cloud stopped people approaching the isolation valves." Which level is this?** [FND-BDD73BA2; EVD-9612]
- Options:
  - Level 1, unsafe acts
  - Level 2, preconditions **(correct)**
  - Level 3, supervision
  - Level 4, organisational influences
- Why it matters: Placing a cause at its level (L1 to L4).
- Answer: Level 2, preconditions: the physical and technological environment at the time. It explains why the act of isolating was delayed, and points to a design change (remote operation).
- Good evidence looks like: The learner links the precondition to an engineering fix.

*(learning: LR-09)*

**Q: Why is more training alone not enough to close the control-room competence gap seen here?** [FND-7885BCF5; EVD-5634]
- Why it matters: Understanding what training cannot fix.
- Answer: There was no defined qualification and no simulator practice, and the valves and alarms needed people to act. Training supports, but does not replace, a qualification standard used for rostering and the engineering changes.
- Good evidence looks like: The learner names one system change that must accompany the training.
