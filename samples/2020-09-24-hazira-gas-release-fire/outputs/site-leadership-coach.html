Bottom line

  • The emergency plan gave the night shift no isolation criteria and no standing authority below the plant manager, so the decision to isolate travelled up a chain of phone calls while the cloud grew. Evidence: corroborated
  • Isolation and detection were set up so that someone had to reach the release: valves closed only by hand at the valve, and detection that only displayed an alarm. Evidence: corroborated

  • Warnings were on record for years (repeat seal changes, pipe-wall loss, an unused change procedure), and none reached a leadership decision that changed the plant. Evidence: direct

Maintenance and spares programme

The maintenance and spares programme, one of the two largest hubs in the causal links, did not supply a specification for the 1986 meter's gasket or a reliability review of repeat failures. Supervision therefore could not act on the risk it saw, rules on the job were not enforced, and checklists were not followed at return to service. The pathway ends on a seal that was not to specification and was never tested to design pressure [; ; ; ; ].

Evidence: corroborated

Culture and risk practice

A culture that allowed unsafe work and applied risk management unevenly leads to gaps in the guidance supervisors gave and to routine deviations at the job. The findings read this as norms, not choices by one person: the substitute O-ring was 'regular practice', and the change procedure was not used after 2018. The pathway ends on changes to the plant that nobody reviewed [; ; ; ].

Culture signals

3

What had become normal

Fitting a locally bought O-ring to a rectangular-slot flange was described as regular practice, repeated across three replacements in about 22 months, with no specification, quality check or recorded decision. Accepting a repaired pressure boundary after an operating-pressure soak had also become the modelled return-to-service practice [; ; ; ; ].

Evidence: corroborated

What was tolerated

Breakdown repair was the accepted approach for flameproof fittings in the Zone 1 area, and twelve external-audit recommendations ran on extended dates with no record of the approval the procedure required. Both were visible in site records [; ; ; ].

Evidence: direct

Where challenge was absent

The emergency structure ran as a serial chain: perimeter security, CCTV room, gas terminal control room, shift in-charge, overall shift in-charge, then plant leadership. No role below the plant manager held standing authority to close the isolation valve, so each step reported up rather than acted [; ; ; ; ].

Evidence: corroborated

Isolation authority

Give shift crews standing written authority to isolate and shut down on criteria written into the emergency plan

Options
  • Shift in-charge isolates on defined criteria and reports afterwards
  • Control-room operator isolates on a confirmed release, shift in-charge confirms
Trade-offs

Delegation removes the escalation delay but means accepting some isolations that later prove unnecessary, with lost production; the leadership team has to back crews who isolate in good faith.
