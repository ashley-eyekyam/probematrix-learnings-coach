Front sheet: decisions and indicators

6

Seal specification

Give one function ownership of every pressure-part specification on the site and of approving any substitute.

Options
  • Maintenance owns the specification and engineering approves substitutes
  • Engineering owns both, with maintenance and stores bound to its specification list
Trade-offs

Ownership in maintenance sits close to the work; ownership in engineering keeps the design authority in one place. Either way the stores issue rule must follow it.

Return to service

Make a test to design pressure, or an engineered equivalent, a hold point before any pressure-boundary joint returns to service.

Options
  • A written procedure with a hold point in the work order
  • The same, with the work-order system refusing release until a test result is entered
Trade-offs

A system block is stronger than a procedure but needs the work-order system changed; both lengthen the return to service.

Repeat failures

Decide how a repeat failure of the same pressure item is escalated, and when an item is replaced rather than repaired again.

Options
  • An automatic repeat-failure trigger in the maintenance system
  • A monthly repeat-failure review owned by a reliability role
Trade-offs

A trigger catches every repeat but needs someone to own the analysis it opens; a review needs a role the site does not have today.

S2 Practice (L1)

5

Fitting the locally bought O-ring had become the normal way to do the job, so it did not feel like a choice.

The substitute was used as a regular practice, with no specification or quality check to compare it against [; ; ]. It made sense at the time: the job had to be done, the original part could not be specified, and nothing in the system said stop.

+3 supporting

With no written test, the crew used the check it had: pressurising to operating pressure.

After the gasket change on 18 September 2020 no hydro test was done; the system was pressurised to operating pressure and the meter went online the next day [; ; ; ]. No procedure named a test pressure, so the quickest available check passed the joint.

S9 Tier messages

5

From the register's tier messages for the seal (LR-01) and the return-to-service test (LR-02).

T1

If you cannot confirm a seal or gasket matches the specification for a pressure part, stop and ask for the spec. Do not fit 'what we normally use'. After any work that opens a pressure boundary, ask: what test proves this joint, and who witnessed it? Write the answer on the job record.

T2

Before signing off pressure-boundary work, check the part against a written specification. No spec means no sign-off. Do not accept equipment back from maintenance without a recorded leak/pressure test to the written test pressure.

T3

Find every pressure-boundary item with no specification on file and re-establish it (OEM, reverse engineering or an engineered substitute). Write and issue a return-to-service procedure for high-pressure equipment that sets test type, test pressure and witness.

T4

Give one named function ownership of pressure-part specifications and of approving any substitute. Treat return to service after pressure-boundary work as a hold point that operations must accept in writing.

T5

Ask for assurance that legacy (pre-2000) pressure equipment has current part specifications and controlled spares.
