## Bottom line

- No specification, no fit: a seal that does not match the original is a change, not a spare. [FND-8C0BB623; EVD-8645] *(learning: LR-01)*
- A joint you opened is not proven until it has passed a recorded test to the written test pressure. [FND-212DC9B7; EVD-2894] *(learning: LR-02)*
- When the same fault comes back, say so on the job record; a repeat is a warning, not a routine job. [FND-69067A1C; EVD-1494] *(learning: LR-05)*

## What happened

On 18 Sep 2020 the seal on a 1986 condensate meter at the gas terminal was changed. The maker's specification had been lost, so a locally bought round O-ring was fitted, as had become regular practice, and the system was brought only to operating pressure, with no test to design pressure [FND-8C0BB623; FND-212DC9B7; EVD-8645; EVD-2894]. The meter went back on line the next day and was in use on 24 Sep, when that seal was the probable source of a release that later caught fire [FND-15279F97; EVD-2894]. *(learning: LR-01)*

## Did you know?

The meter was built for a rectangular-slot gasket; the round O-ring was a local substitute, bought with no specification or quality check [FND-7A32AC87; EVD-8645]. *(learning: LR-01)*

This was the third seal change on the same meter in about 22 months, and a malfunction notification had been raised the day before the last one. None of the repeats led to a review of why it kept leaking [FND-69067A1C; FND-82FC718D; EVD-1494; EVD-0591]. *(learning: LR-05)*

No written procedure covered testing these high-pressure meters before they went back into service, so holding at operating pressure became the normal check [FND-257C1100; EVD-2894]. *(learning: LR-02)*

## What you can do

- [ ] Check the part against the groove and the specification before you fit it. If nobody can show a specification, stop and ask the supervisor. *(before fitting a seal or gasket on a pressure part)* [FND-89962239; EVD-8645] *(learning: LR-01)*
- [ ] **Hold point:** Before you hand the equipment back, ask for the written test pressure and the witness, and write the test result on the job record; if no test was done, write that. *(before return to service)* [FND-15279F97; EVD-2894] *(learning: LR-02)*
- [ ] Write "repeat failure" on the job record when you fix the same fault again, and tell the supervisor how often you have seen it. *(when a fault comes back)* [FND-69067A1C; EVD-1494] *(learning: LR-05)*

## Questions for the crew

**Q: Which pressure parts on our unit have no specification we can find?**
- Why it matters: The seal on this meter had no specification on file, so a substitute became the normal part [FND-B356F15B; EVD-8645].
- Answer: A good answer names the parts, where their specification should be kept, and who the crew asks when a spare differs from the original.

*(learning: LR-01)*

**Q: What test proves a joint we have just made, and where is the result written?**
- Why it matters: After the seal change the system was only brought to operating pressure, with no test to design pressure [FND-7C5ED5B3; EVD-2894].
- Answer: A good answer gives the test, the test pressure, who witnesses it and where the result is recorded before operations accept the equipment.

*(learning: LR-02)*

**Q: Which items do we keep fixing, and who decides whether we repair again or look for the cause?**
- Why it matters: Repeat seal failures on this meter were handled as routine repairs [FND-82FC718D; EVD-1494].
- Answer: A good answer names the items, says where a repeat would show on the job record, and names the role that starts a cause review.

*(learning: LR-05)*
