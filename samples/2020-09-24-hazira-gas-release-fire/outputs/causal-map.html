Bottom line

  • Two organisational hubs feed the most links: a culture that allowed unsafe tasks (OIC 2.1, 36 links out) and a maintenance, spares and support programme that did not hold (OIR 1.6, 26 links out). Links out are a count of links, not a measure of strength. Evidence: direct
  • The pathway that matters most runs from the seal specification to the leak: no one owned the specification, supervision had no rule to enforce, the job had no guidance, and the joint went back into service on an unspecified O-ring without a test to design pressure. Evidence: corroborated
  • Inconsistent risk management of known problems (OIC 2.7) links to supervision not responding to critical information (SVI 1.6): repeated seal failures and an open notification did not start a root-cause review. Evidence: direct
  • After the release, the chain runs through command and control resources (OIR 1.1) that did not support isolation: valves that could be closed only by hand at the valve, gas detection that alarmed without acting, and an emergency plan with no isolation criteria. Evidence: corroborated

Causal pathways

6

Each pathway reads top-down, one sentence per level, and ends on the condition at the sharp end.

Seal specification to leak

No role owned the meter gasket specification or the authority to approve a substitute, and the original specification was not on file (L4) [; ; ]. Supervision had no specification, procurement or quality-control rule to enforce, so the locally bought O-ring became the regular repair part (L3) [; ]. At the job, the specification and a quality check for the part were not available (L2) []. The 1986 flange, machined for a rectangular-slot gasket, was resealed on 18 September 2020 with an unspecified round O-ring (L1) [; ].

Evidence: corroborated

Return to service without a test

No procedure set how high-pressure meters were tested and returned to service after maintenance (L4) [; ]. Pressurising to operating pressure had become the modelled norm, and the missing hydrostatic test was not picked up before the meter went back online (L3) [; ]. The repaired joint returned to service with a substitute seal and no hydrotest (L2) []. The joint was proved only at operating pressure and was in use when it failed (L1) [; ].

Evidence: direct

Repeat failures read as routine

Recurring gasket failures on the meter were treated as routine maintenance, and the risk of the pattern was never assessed (L4) [; ; ]. Supervision had no trigger to turn three gasket replacements in 22 months into a root-cause investigation (L3) [; ]. The chronically failing meter stayed in service across the replacements (L2) []. Each failure was handled as a fresh repair with the same substitute part, so the pattern produced no change (L1) [].

Evidence: direct

Isolation waiting for authority

The on-site disaster management plan covered hydrocarbon leakage but gave no isolation or stoppage criteria and no delegated authority to isolate (L4) [; ]. With no standard criteria, each responder improvised and the decision travelled up the night-shift chain (L3) [; ]. A steep authority gradient left people at the scene waiting for direction (L2) [; ]. The first emergency isolation valve was closed 46 minutes after the leak was first detected (L1) [; ].

Evidence: corroborated

Valves out of reach

The emergency isolation valves were bought and kept as local-manual valves, never upgraded for remote operation from the control room (L4) [; ; ]. That design was left uncorrected (L3) []. Dense vapour around the leak stopped anyone approaching the source or the valves (L2) [; ]. Control-room staff could not carry out the isolation from where they could safely stand (L1) [].

Evidence: corroborated

Detection that informs rather than acts

The fire and gas detection system was provided as an annunciator display with no automatic shutdown or suppression, and was never upgraded (L4) [; ; ]. No policy required it to be interlocked (L3) []. The detection lagged the people who saw the cloud by 5 minutes and left a fully manual response (L2) [; ]. The alarm at 02:20 started a manual search, not a protective action (L1) [; ].

Evidence: corroborated
