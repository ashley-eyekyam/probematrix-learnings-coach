Bottom line

  • Warnings were on record from 2011 onwards, and each one stalled at a step of the learning cycle rather than with one person.
  • Reporting: repeat seal failures on the meter that later leaked were closed as routine jobs and never reported as a warning.
  • Planning and intervening: known wall loss had no corrective plan, and overdue audit actions were extended without the approval the procedure required.
  • Evaluating: no step checked that a job or a drill had worked, so the system never learned that it had not.

Missed opportunities

1

Eleven dated warnings from 2011 to the week of the release, each tied to a finding that cites an earlier recommendation or a known problem left uncorrected. A year-only date means the source gives only the year.

  1. c. 2011 (about 9 years before)Weak signal Pipe-wall thickness measured in 2011, and next in 2017, against a four-year schedule.

    Response: None recorded: the slipped schedule was not escalated.

    Knowable then: The schedule and the dates of the last measurements were in the inspection records.

  2. 2016-08 (49 months before)Weak signal The management-of-change procedure is published.

    Response: Not applied to the 2018 header change, and not used for any change after that.

    Knowable then: The procedure was a published site document.

  3. c. 2018 (about 2 years before)Weak signal A gas header change is made without the management-of-change procedure.

    Response: None recorded: no change after this went through it.

    Knowable then: The change records would show whether a change went through the procedure.

  4. 2018-02 (31 months before)Weak signal External safety audit: most recommendations closed, the rest given later target dates.

    Response: Target dates extended to 30/04/2021 with no record of the approval the procedure required.

    Knowable then: The audit report and its open recommendations were on record.

  5. 2018-11 (22 months before)Weak signal Meters 1 and 2 cleaned, serviced and calibrated: the first entry in the recorded seal history of meter 1.

    Response: Closed as routine maintenance.

    Knowable then: The job was in the maintenance history.

  6. c. 2019 (about 1 year before)Weak signal The internal safety audit again records observations without a filled checklist.

    Response: None recorded.

    Knowable then: The audit report showed that no checklist was filled.

  7. 2019-02-25 (18 months before)Weak signal Meter 1 cleaned and its gasket changed again.

    Response: Closed as routine maintenance; no root-cause review recorded.

    Knowable then: The repeat job on the same meter was in the maintenance history.

  8. 2019-07 (14 months before)Weak signal Heavy pitting and wall loss found on a segment of the 36-inch pipeline.

How practice drifted

2

Fitting a locally bought O-ring in place of the original gasket became the regular practice on meter 1. Each seal job solved the day's leak and was closed as routine, so the repeat never looked like a change that needed review.

+7 supporting

What the documents said and what the site did moved apart in small steps: a change procedure that stopped being used, drill results kept in an informal parallel record, and audits that recorded compliance without the data behind it. No single decision made the gap.

Learnings for the organisation

6

A repeat failure on the same item needs a route that reports it as a warning, not as another job. Owner: Mechanical Maintenance (F02, T3)

The seal jobs on meter 1 were each recorded and closed as routine maintenance [; ; ]. The analysis states that the risk of the recurring failure was never assessed []: no step in the maintenance records turned the repeats into a review.

Evidence: corroborated
+3 supporting

An audit that records no checklist cannot find what it did not look at, so its silence is not assurance. Owner: HSE, Process Safety & Audit (F11, T3)

The 2018 and 2019 internal audits gave observations without a filled checklist [; ], and the self-audit checklist was generic to the location [; ]. Without a record of what was checked, the audits could not turn the plant's defects into findings.

Evidence: direct

A finding that needs corrective action needs a plan with an owner, or measurement becomes the response. Owner: Asset Integrity, Corrosion & Inspection (F05, T3)

The 2017 thickness report called for corrective action, and three years later no plan existed [; ; ]. When wall loss was found on the 36-inch line, painting was accepted as the fix without examining whether it was enough [; ].

Evidence: direct
+3 supporting

Extending an overdue safety action needs the approval the procedure sets, or the extension becomes the norm. Owner: Plant & Asset Leadership (F13, T4)

Most recommendations of the February 2018 external audit were complied with; the rest were extended to 30/04/2021 with no record of the required approval [; ; ]. No record shows that the role with that authority accepted the delay.

Evidence: direct
+4 supporting

A published procedure is a learning only while it is used; when it falls out of use, the change it guarded goes unreviewed. Owner: Engineering, Design, Documents & MOC (F07, T3)

The management-of-change procedure was published in August 2016 and, as the record states, no change went through it after 2018 [; ; ]. The substitute seal on meter 1 was therefore never reviewed as a change [].

Evidence: direct
+2 supporting

The learning cycle closes only when someone checks that a job or an exercise did what it was for. Owner: Work Management & Planning (F08, T3)

The notification for meter 1 was not closed or reviewed after the gasket job, and no role was assigned to review it [; ; ]. Fire-drill records held no actions or observations [; ], so neither could show whether anything had improved.

Evidence: direct
+8 supporting

A note on hindsight

1

This review knows that meter 1 leaked on 24 September 2020; the people at the time did not. Every missed opportunity says what the record showed at that moment, events whose meaning was clear only afterwards are left out, and each learning asks why the system did not turn a warning into a change, not who missed it.

Limits of this pack

  • The missed opportunities are the warnings the export dates; undated warnings in the findings are in the cycle table and the learnings only.
  • Earlier incidents at other sites are not in this export; the review covers this site's own records.
  • Some findings cite a recommendation of the post-incident committee rather than an earlier one; those are not treated as missed opportunities, because nobody could act on them before the event.
